Red Box

Five Budgets, 2026 to 2031, then they vote. Six levers, two of which pay off after you have gone, and a forecast that will be revised underneath you. Balance the books if you can.

  1. 1Five Budgets, then a general election One Budget a year. The election is the result — the score is only how you got there.
  2. 2Six levers, three each side Income tax and NI, VAT, corporation tax. Day-to-day spending, welfare, capital investment. Each one is a change to the standing level, in £bn a year.
  3. 3A giveaway only works once This year's increase is next year's baseline. The boost goes; the bill stays.
  4. 4The Bank is watching Loosen too hard and the Monetary Policy Committee raises Bank Rate on you. It cannot be overruled — you are not the Governor here.
  5. 5The bond market prices the surprise Borrow more than expected while debt is rising and gilt yields move. Debt interest then eats the headroom you were spending.
  6. 6The fiscal rule is the hard constraint Debt must be falling as a share of GDP by 2031. The forecast that judges you gets revised, and not usually in your favour.
